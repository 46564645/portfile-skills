## 0. Format and Visual System
- 6 ultra-wide spreads (~2.83:1) plus an overview strip.
- Type: speculative interactive installation (heart-rate sensor + servo-moved moss + screen visuals, Arduino).
- Ground: muted sage green; SKETCH/CODING spread switches to dark olive; TIMELINE uses off-white paper texture.
- Accent: yellow-green / moss for headings and highlight bars; deep ink-green label chips.
- Level-1 headings: yellow-green serif caps with an offset "ghost" double image.
- Collage language: desaturated industrial images (excavator, refinery, dam, workers) against coloured nature (moss, otter, waves), over old German newspaper and torn paper; red pins, plaster hands, light bulbs and postmarks as props. The collage itself states the human-vs-nature conflict.

## 1. Logic Chain
Cover (what it is + one-line concept) → INSPIRATION (two metaphors, "Fast Water" vs "Slow Water") → BACKGROUND (5–6 global ecological crises with sources) → TIMELINE (human activity vs natural change across four eras) → INTERVIEW (three people who all feel the crisis is "far from me") → THEORY (moss symbolism + circadian/body rhythms + ecosystem resilience) → DESIGN CONCEPT + user flow (heartbeat drives moss; two states) → SKETCH → CODING & TESTING → FINAL OUTPUT.
Bridge: interviewees feel distant → the crisis must become bodily → body rhythm (heartbeat) drives nature rhythm (moss). The insight is never stated as its own block; the reader must connect it.

## 2. Page-by-Page Description
**P1 Cover + INSPIRATION.** Left: desaturated photo of the build (monitor with a light bar on top, marble-texture screen, a mat of moss on the desk) under the ghosted title "NATURE BEATING" and the line "PERSONAL PROJECT| SPECULATIVE INSTALLATION DESIGN", plus a translucent green intro card. Right: INSPIRATION with two definition cards — Fast Water (rapid, urgent, uncontrollable) and Slow Water (calm, stable; label hidden by a logo) — over a collage of waterfall, otter, B/W wave, dam and grass.

**P2 BACKGROUND.** World-map outline on old newsprint; foreground strip left→right: tree, cut logs, excavator, overflowing bin, man with fishing rod, refinery towers, mossy log. Green cards with causal titles, e.g. "Amazon Rainforest Deforestation Leads To Biodiversity Loss", "Plastic Pollution Exacerbates Marine Ecological Crisis", "Overgrazing Leads To Global Rangeland Degradation"; bodies cite WWF, UNCCD, UNEP and FAO with bolded figures.

**P3 TIMELINE.** Paper ground with a horizontal ink-green band and four era tags: ANCIENT TIMES → THE MIDDLE AGES → INDUSTRIAL REVOLUTION → MODERN TIMES. Above/below alternate "Human Activities" (green) and "Natural Changes" (pale yellow) cards, pinned with red pins and held by plaster hands; images run from early humans and canoes to factories. Defect: the "Period:" tag text is identical under all four eras, and two Human Activities cards repeat the same text (copy-paste errors).

**P4 INTERVIEW + THEORY + DESIGN CONCEPT (three columns).** Interview: three B/W cut-outs with speech bubbles and ink-green name plates — an urban office worker (32, Beijing), a building-materials CEO (45, Guangzhou), an environmental-science student (21, Nanjing); all three express distance or cost barriers. Theory: a triangle linking MOSS (purity / undisturbed nature in Taoist and Nordic culture), CIRCADIAN RHYTHMS (body rhythms resonate with Earth cycles) and ECOSYSTEM RESILIENCE THEORY, with moss forest and an anatomical heart in the middle. Concept: a highlight card (respect nature's inherent laws, using fast/slow water) and a user flow: Connects (finger on pulse sensor) → Showing Visual Graphics → Screen Reaction; Sound Detection; "Slow Water State — Moss Still Normal / Heart Beats Normally" (sound within a threshold) vs "Fast Water State — The Moss Move Rapidly / Heart Beats Disorderly" (above it).

**P5 SKETCH + CODING & TESTING (olive ground).** Left: white line drawing of a man at a moss-covered desk facing a heart on a wall screen, labelled Screen, Pulse Sensor, Servo Motor, Moss, Arduino Uno, with dashed leaders to real component photos. Right: a code screenshot frame and five B/W test photos (serial data on a laptop, sensor, servo, hand-held test). "Testing" here means component debugging, not user testing.

**P6 FINAL OUTPUT.** Large colour hero: monitor showing teal fluid texture with a red heart, bright moss in front, a "VIDEO LINK:" placeholder. Right grid: "Normal State" (green fluid + red heart), "Moss & Motor", "Heart Rate Sensor", "Abnormal State" (purple-pink fluid + black cracked heart), "Interactive Behaviour" (palm on screen, glowing sensor, a face talking to the screen).

## 3. Block-Writing Patterns
- Metaphor first: define two opposed concepts on page 1 and return to them on every page.
- Background card title = "X Leads To / Exacerbates Y" + institution + bold figure.
- Timeline = era × paired "Human Activities / Natural Changes" cards.
- Interview = name + identity/age/place + bubble quote; deliberately pick opposing stances that converge on one insight.
- Theory = three-node relationship diagram (symbol + body rhythm + ecology theory).
- Interaction mapping = state name + material behaviour + body behaviour + threshold.

## 4. Why It Works
1. One metaphor (fast/slow water) runs through concept, timeline and the two final states.
2. Collage carries meaning (B/W industry vs colour nature), not decoration.
3. Strict three-green palette plus paper keeps image-heavy pages calm.
4. Clear hierarchy: ghost heading → H2 bar → card title bar → body → highlighted phrase.
5. Final page pairs Normal vs Abnormal states, so the interaction result reads instantly.

## 5. Weaknesses
- No audience testing, iteration or reflection.
- Copy-paste errors in the timeline; placeholder video link.
- Insight/HMW not stated as a block.
- Small text over collage; very low post resolution.
