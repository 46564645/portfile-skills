## 0. Format and Visual System
- About 16 half-spreads (landscape) shown as four 2×2 grids, plus a low-res overview.
- Type: product family (three beehives) + app + brand + service blueprint + business model.
- Cream ground; main colour honey orange / caramel; secondary deep navy for the big "BUZZ" and section names.
- **Motif: the hexagon** — stakeholder map cells, dashboard data cards, the community hive's lattice shell, the bee logo.
- Navy bold caps section names; orange bold-italic sub-titles; key phrases in **orange all-caps** inside grey body.
- Components: orange "Design point N" cards; line drawing with numbered leaders opposite a render; orange-italic "Conclusion" blocks.

## 1. Logic Chain
Cover (three-product family + description) → BACKGROUND (why bees matter → why wild bees decline in China → population curve → social vs solitary bees → body size and nesting) → STAKEHOLDER MAP + conclusion → INTERVIEW (3) + conclusion → DESIGN POSITIONING → DESIGN CONCEPT (product–app–brand network) → MOODBOARD → SKETCH → MATERIAL & COLOR → PRODUCT DESIGN ×3 → UI DESIGN (wireframes, logo, visual norms, hi-fi) → SERVICE BLUEPRINT → BUSINESS CANVAS → DESIGN DISPLAY → Before/After conclusion.
Key move: **the biological classification (social vs solitary bees) directly generates the product variants** (household, community, solitary hive). The background research is actually used.

## 2. Page-by-Page Description
**Cover.** Left third: huge navy "BUZZ" and a four-sentence description (what it does → what happens without it → what beekeeping adds → benefits for two user types). Right two-thirds: render of all three products on yellow organic shapes — small white/orange household hive, large white hexagonal-lattice community hive with a side door open on drawer frames, orange columnar solitary hive.

**BACKGROUND.** Left: three orange pill labels (ecological balance, agricultural yield, pollinator) with hexagon icons; three reasons wild bees decline; a curve where total numbers rise but only because of kept bees — the reversal sentence is highlighted in caps (strongest insight line). Right: a honey-drip central axis comparing SOCIAL BEE vs SOLITARY BEE traits side by side.

**STAKEHOLDER MAP.** Hexagon grid with "Urban Ecology" at the centre and government, community, beekeeper, brand, hive, app, user etc. in surrounding cells (blank cells as rhythm). Below, a Conclusion table sorts primary / secondary / tertiary stakeholders. No connecting lines, so value flows are invisible.

**INTERVIEW.** Three people in a Z layout (community resident, young urban resident, professional beekeeper): cut-out, orange question bubble, identity card, quote with only key phrases in orange caps (fear of stings, need for expertise, want community support, new income). Conclusion sorts needs into shared direct / unique direct / indirect.

**DESIGN POSITIONING.** Three columns — Sustainability, Society participation, Communicability — each with an icon and two verb-led sub-goals plus one sentence. Safety (the biggest interview fear) is missing here.

**DESIGN CONCEPT.** Triangle network of Product design / App / Brand with satellite nodes (hive types, positioning, app functions). Tiny text and crossing lines; reads like a thinking sketch. A price in pounds clashes with the China context.

**MOODBOARD + SKETCH.** Six un-captioned images (planters, glass dome, community gardening, bamboo bee hotel, hexagonal wood building). Three hand-sketch groups (household, community, solitary), each with numbered options ①–⑦; the chosen option sits on a pale brown disc — divergence → convergence at a glance, but no selection rationale.

**MATERIAL & COLOR.** HDPE with one reason sentence (UV, durability, corrosion); a three-circle Venn of bee-preferred colours (blue, white, yellow) with a reason. The final products are white + orange, unexplained.

**PRODUCT DESIGN ×3 (same template).** Left: isometric line drawing with numbered dots and leader notes ("The [part] can be used to / prevents / allows…" — e.g. top hollow for nectar plants, monitoring interface, water filter, glass door for honey, solar panel). Right: render + "Main module of product" layer labels + angled orange "Design statement" + 2–3 "Design point N" cards; some pages add an explosion view.

**UI DESIGN.** Left: a wall of ~40 wireframes linked by flow lines (emergency call steps, map, beehive data pages, social, courses, settings). Centre: logo formula (infinity = sustainability + cloud = online + bee) and visual norms. Right: hi-fi screens — hive status ("The hive is in good condition!" with humidity/temperature/count), video, shop, map booking, an SOS emergency slider (answers the sting fear), social.

**SERVICE BLUEPRINT.** Three illustrated named journeys, one per product (home, community event, solitary-hive visit), above orange swimlanes: Touchpoint / Front-end / Back-end services. No emotion curve or pain points.

**BUSINESS CANVAS.** Standard nine boxes; the cost box carries a wrong title (copy error).

**DESIGN DISPLAY.** Three full-bleed rendered scenes (balcony, community green, park) each combining the product, a translucent human silhouette and the matching app screen, with a caption; the third caption is copied from another.

**Conclusion.** Symmetric Before/After four-petal diagrams (poor service, low awareness … → better service, elevated awareness …) joined by an "Urban beekeeping" pill; projected, not measured.

## 3. Block-Writing Patterns
- Background: caps phenomenon label + causal paragraph + **one reversal sentence** in caps highlight.
- Interview: question bubble → identity → quote with highlighted key phrases → conclusion sorted by shared/unique × direct/indirect needs.
- Positioning: category noun + two verb-led sub-goals.
- Product: "Design statement" ("A beehive designed specifically for X, allowing …") + "Design point N" (noun title + part → need).
- Annotation: number + "The [part] … can be used to / prevents / allows …".
- Journey title: person's name + scenario ("Jack's Home Beekeeping Journey").

## 4. Why It Works
1. **Research classification → product variants**: social/solitary biology → home/community/park scenes → three hives.
2. One reusable product-page template (line notes + render + modules + design points + explosion).
3. Motif and colour run from charts to product form.
4. Full system: product, app, brand, service, business, scenes, before/after.
5. Highlighted keywords keep dense pages scannable.

## 5. Weaknesses
1. No prototype, user testing or iteration; the conclusion is a projection.
2. Very dense, tiny text; grammar and copy errors.
3. Research→design breaks: colour research ignored; "safety" not in positioning; currency mismatch.
4. AI-style renders with silhouettes instead of real use.

## 6. Completeness
Fairly complete system portfolio, missing validation. Missing: emotion journey/persona, HMW or principles, selection rationale, physical prototype and dimensions, usability testing, iteration, measured impact.
