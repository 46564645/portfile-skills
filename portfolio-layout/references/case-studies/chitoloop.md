## 0. Format and Global Visual System

- **Format**: 9 landscape spreads, 1684 × 595 (about 2.83:1).
- **Project type**: material experimentation (chitosan from crayfish shells) + circular service design + aquaculture.
- **Narrative voice**: "I". Most pages open with a line of the form "To + purpose, I + action".
- **Background**: pale sage-green or off-white pages; real pond, plant and tank photographs.
- **Single accent family**: deep forest green for headings, lines and chart strokes. Light-green fills mark research zones and the "success / conclusion" block (Test 3, for example). Red "!" marks problems; yellow "!" marks the risk months; a green ✓ marks the chosen option.
- **Typography as observed**: large bold serif all-caps headings (e.g. "ELODEA RESEARCH", "FIELD OBSERVATION"), italic serif sub-headings (e.g. "Essential Biological Functions", "Multi-Species Strategy", "Conclusion"), and serif body text. Section labels are nouns; sub-titles are often questions ("Where oversupply hurts？").
- **Recurring components**: "Conclusion" blocks at the end of each zone; Q1–Q3 interview stacks; capsule-shaped takeaways; Test rows; photo matrices; numbered task cards with a "Feedback" line; a phased roadmap.
- **Naming**: "CHITOLOOP" (title), "ChitoLoop" (cover body), ""Chitoloop"" (page 09); "Chitosan Disks" vs "Disk Poles" / "disk poles module" inside body text.

### Page 05 — Service Workflow / Substrate Experiment
- **Grid**: service workflow on the left ~35%; experiment on the right ~65% (stock-dope column + three test rows).
- **Service workflow**: "To clarify chitosan sourcing and build a practical, compliant circular chain, I mapped the service workflow."
  - "Where does Chitosan come from？" — "Mainly from crustacean shells (crab, shrimp, crayfish; also lobster/krill)."
  - "Scope set： crayfish shells" — "1. Closed-loop with target industry→ stakeholder alignment" / "2.Local abundance (Hubei) with peak May–Aug."
  - Excluded: "Restaurant Food Waste" ⊗, "Restaurant kitchen waste is typically government -regulated and centrally collected in China."
  - Loop diagram: Cold Product Factory / Farm co-ops → Shell Recycle → Collection Hub (sorting & storage) → Chitosan extractor (industrial partner) → Module Produce (+ "By-product (CaCO₃) to make bases") → Logistics provider → Installer (pond works) → Deploy in ponds (Jun-Aug) → Field O&M (replace regularly) → Module recycling → back to the hub; outputs "Higher-Quality Crayfish"; "Researchers observe and improve".
  - Caption: "A compliant circular chain links factories, farms, collection, extraction, manufacturing, logistics, and pond O&M; modules deploy among elodea and are later recycled to stabilize summer habitat."
- **Substrate experiment**: "To identify a workable forming route and the final material for the module, I ran a series of chitosan substrate tests."
  - "Stock Dope（for all tests）": "Add chitosan to 1% acetic acid" → "Add glycerin" → "Stir for 2 to 4 hours until clear" → beaker "Chitosan solution 2% (w/v)".
  - "Test 1 - Air-dry Thick Casting (failed)": "Pour into a Petri dish to a 3–4 mm layer" → "Air-dry 24–36 h at room temperature" → "demold and briefly rehydrate to check strength" → "Edges warped, center tacky; it swelled/softened and lost shape". Mechanism: "Drying alone forms a reversible network with no crosslinking; the acidic film rehydrates, it quickly returns to a sticky gel and lacks strength."
  - "Test 2 - STPP Sheet Casting (failed)": "1.5% STPP (w/v)", "Water 250 ml + STPP 3.25 g", "Pour the STPP solution onto the upper layer of the chitosan solution" → "Did not gel as a continuous sheet center remained liquid". Mechanism: "Top-cast layer was diluted at the interface; ionic strength/diffusion insufficient → center stayed liquid."
  - "Test 3 - NaOH Block Neutralization (success)" on a light-green row: "NaOH 4% (w/v)", "Submerge or top-cover", "Flip every 10–15 min", "Rinse to pH≈7" → "Substrate". Summary: "Air-dry casting failed (rehydrated); STPP sheet still didn't gel through. NaOH neutralization produced a robust bulk gel. In water it can slowly surface-erodes, releasing trace chitosan to the root zone and stabilizing summer conditions." This links back to page 04's "root-zone slow release".
- **Weaknesses**: two big topics on one page; tiny icons and text; leftover "landscape design." layer; the system appears before the material is proven, which makes the order feel slightly jumpy.

## 3. Block-Writing Formulas

| Block | Formula | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Intro line | To + purpose, I + action | "To identify a workable forming route…, I ran a series of chitosan substrate tests." |
| Reframing | X produce what markets don't want (n%) and can't produce enough of what markets demand (n%) | The Core Problem |
| Interview drill-down | Q1 → Q2 → Q3, bold keywords, then "The Critical Discovery" | "The biggest problem is **summer heat** killing our **elodea**." |
| Time window | Core risk window = period: demand peaks while supply bottoms | page 03 Conclusion |
| Folk-rule target | Quote the rule + turn it into a numeric range | "look at the plants to know the yield… 50–60%" |
| Co-creation funnel | Role quote → one design instruction → ≤ 3 attributes with dimensions | "Clustered vertical blades (18–30 cm)… 1–2 cm gaps" |

## 4. Strengths, Weaknesses, Transferable Lessons

**Why it works**
1. A real reframing, backed by data: oversupply → size mismatch → summer Elodea death. The intervention point is small and the payoff is large.
2. The causal chain crosses three scales (market → ecology → material) and returns to income.
3. Users take part throughout: interview → three-role co-creation → field trial with the same farmer.
4. Honest failures with mechanisms make the success believable.
5. The strongest quantitative evidence of the three cases: 72/48, 78/64, 77%/27%, 46 g/18 g.
6. The name is the concept: the "Loop" promised on the cover is delivered by the service chain (page 05) and the roadmap (page 09).
7. One green family + serif headings + real pond photos give a strong, consistent tone; light-green fills let readers scan for results.
8. Reading only the intro lines and Conclusions retells the whole story.

**Weaknesses to avoid**: typos and inconsistent names; two percentage errors; hidden placeholder layers; small samples with strong wording; projected and measured numbers styled alike; cover merged with background; overloaded pages 02/03/05; no data sources; no competitor or existing-substitute comparison beyond the interview mentions of Hydrilla and bamboo poles.

**Transferable lessons**
- Give every reframing an evidence chart and a contrast sentence.
- Plot supply and demand on one time axis and mark the gap as the design's window.
- Turn users' rules of thumb into numeric targets.
- Run co-creation as a funnel ending in ≤ 3 parameterised attributes.
- Write "Scope set" with two reasons and one justified exclusion.
- Show failed tests with mechanisms; tint the success and link it back to the requirement.
- Compare options with identical frames and paired big numbers; reassign the loser a role.
- In controlled tests list the controls, show group × time, and check every percentage.
- Return to the original interviewee for field tests and capture informative feedback or adoption behaviour.
- Style projections differently from results and give their basis; name partners in roadmaps.
