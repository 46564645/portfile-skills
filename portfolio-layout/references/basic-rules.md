# Basic Rules

Use for spacing, alignment, paragraph, color, page size, margins, headers/footers, section pages, and basic page structure.

## Source Coverage

From Tip items 01, 04, 14, 18, 19, 20, 21, and related basic rules in 15.

## Spacing System: 4/8 Multiples

- Use 4px multiples for small spacing: 4, 8, 12, 16px. Apply to line spacing, icon gaps, micro-components, small labels, and local group spacing.
- Use 8px multiples for larger spacing: 24, 32, 40, 48px. Apply to section gaps, page outer spacing, major title spacing, and block separation.
- Build a portfolio-wide spacing rule so different projects feel like one system, not unrelated slides.
- Exception: expressive collage or special visual treatments can break the strict grid, but the collage as a whole should still align to the larger page system.
- In Figma, use Option on Mac or Alt on Windows to inspect distances.

## Page Size and Page Count

- A commonly recommended digital size is 1920 x 1080, suitable for horizontal portfolio reading and screen presentation.
- Avoid pages that are too narrow or too wide; the page ratio should support reading rather than only spectacle.
- A single main project should usually have at least 4 pages; "other works" can be shorter.
- Even page counts are often easier for combined page display and application review.
- Interaction portfolio guidance from item 21 also mentions one project usually not exceeding about 5 pages and full portfolios often around 15-30 pages, depending on school requirements.

## Page Margins and Text Area

- Treat margins as negative space/留白, not leftover blankness.
- For content-heavy pages, 15mm equal margins are a practical default.
- Keep the page center/版心 consistent across pages. In visual research pages, first define the page ratio, then draw a central content area before placing elements.
- Do not push headers, footers, page numbers, or labels too close to the edge.

## Paragraph and Typography Basics

- For multiple paragraphs, keep paragraph format unified and body text left-aligned.
- Body text should be concise. Avoid explaining everything; prioritize innovation, key insight, and decision logic.
- Item 20 recommends clean sans-serif families such as 思源黑体, 苹方, or Helvetica for a cleaner portfolio tone.
- Establish clear text roles: project title, section title, key statement, body, caption, label.

## Color Rules

- One page should not use too many colors. Too many colors scatter attention.
- Single-color families, analogous colors, and controlled complementary colors are safer than unrelated palettes.
- Item 20 recommends no more than about three main colors and lower-saturation palettes as a safer choice.
- Item 14 shows a practical method: unify pictures, titles, icons, and lines into one visual language; weaken the background so important information emerges.
- For academic or social issue pages, a gray/black base plus one accent color can create restraint and focus.

## Charts and Infographics

- Use visual charts for data or change over time, not long numerical paragraphs.
- Infographics help reviewers quickly scan information and can also improve visual quality.
- Choose a chart form based on the relationship: comparison, process, network, hierarchy, system, or evidence chain.

## Headers, Footers, and Section Pages

- Headers often sit top-left or top-right, outside the main content area.
- Footers usually sit bottom-left or bottom-center; right-side footers can work when the page system supports it.
- Section pages act like small covers between projects. Prefer one relevant image plus limited text.
- Avoid overloading section pages with many images or heavy explanations, or they lose their separating function.

## 3/1 Cutting Principle

- Divide pages around a 1/3 and 2/3 relationship rather than splitting mechanically in half.
- Avoid exact 1/2 splits, overly symmetrical structures, visual imbalance, and overly fragmented blocks.
- Use 1/3 for secondary information and 2/3 for the main visual or core content, or reverse this when the text block is the conceptual anchor.

## Process Timing

- Do not wait until every project is complete before thinking about layout.
- Begin layout planning around the middle of the project so the page requirements can push you to create missing diagrams, process evidence, or final images.
